Core Problems

The Gender Violence Database is an open-access initiative founded nearly a decade ago by Professor Jami Ake at WashU’s Humanities Digital Workshop, an interdisciplinary lab that pairs faculty and students on digital humanities research. Over the years, the project has grown through student-led data labeling and close partnerships with local gender advocacy organizations.

Despite the depth of this community-built archive, an outdated interface and fragmented content structure prevented researchers from easily discovering and parsing the data.

  • Opaque search and navigation

    The legacy architecture hid the scope of the archive, forcing users to rely on exact searches rather than organic browsing.

  • Low visual clarity

    Flat hierarchy and inconsistent layouts made dense academic text difficult to scan, calling for a sharper visual system that balanced readability with scholarly authority.

Redesign highlights

Leading both UI/UX design and front-end development, I overhauled the site’s visual identity and information architecture. I hand-coded the new interface in HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript—using AI-assisted debugging to accelerate development—and partnered with engineers to integrate it into the existing Vue codebase.

Logo redesign

Alongside the interface, I restructured the GVD logo into a simpler, more regular mark and paired it with a serif wordmark for the site’s navigation.
